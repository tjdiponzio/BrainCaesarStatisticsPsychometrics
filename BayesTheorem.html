What is Bayes' theorem?

Bayes Theorem is a way of calculating probability while taking into consideration the previous knowledge we have about a phenomenon. Based on conditional evidence. It was discovered by Thomas Bayes and developed independently by Pierre-Simon Laplace in the 18th century. It is proven to be mathematically true. It is not a theory or a hypothesis it is proven to be true. It has been used in genetics, brain imaging, and, importantly, for medical diagnosis

Start with the prior (prevalence), weigh it by the likelihood of a positive screen (sensitivity) and the overall evidence (how often the screen is positive for anyone, this involves specificity and sensitivity), and you get the posterior: the chance the client has the condition given a positive screen, also called positive predictive value (PPV). For example, with 85% sensitivity, 90% specificity, and 20% prevalence, a positive screen means about a 68% chance of the condition, not 85%. These terms were simplified for use with Mental Health Practioners.
